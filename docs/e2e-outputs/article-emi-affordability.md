# How much EMI can I afford?

Checking your eligibility for our loans is a soft-check process and will have no impact on your credit score. Determining your repayment capacity is the most critical step before applying for any credit.

Rather than deciding on a loan amount based solely on your immediate needs, you should evaluate your monthly budget to ensure your equated monthly instalments (EMI) remain manageable. Understanding that an EMI includes both a portion of the principal amount and the accrued interest allows you to plan your finances effectively.

## Assess your loan amount and determine how much EMI you can afford before deciding

A common mistake is to decide on the loan amount based solely on your need, such as when renovating a home. However, your ability to manage Equated Monthly Instalments (EMI)—which include principal and interest—is critical to your financial well-being.

To borrow responsibly, you must look at your entire financial landscape. Following these steps will help you calculate a sustainable monthly outflow and strike a balance between meeting your financial obligations and maintaining your lifestyle.

- Evaluate your monthly income: Begin by understanding your total take-home salary, bonuses, and any additional revenue streams. This forms the foundation for calculating a manageable EMI.
- Assess fixed expenses: Take stock of your non-negotiable monthly costs, including rent, utilities, groceries, and insurance premiums. Deduct these from your income to determine the surplus available for loan repayments.
- Consider variable expenses: Factor in discretionary spending like dining out and entertainment. It is advisable to average out a year’s worth of these costs to determine a realistic monthly figure.
- Calculate debt obligations: List all current loan repayments and credit card dues to understand your existing commitments. This helps you see how much additional EMI room you have.
- Determine savings goals: Ensure you are still allocating funds toward your long-term savings and emergency buffers. This maintains your financial security.
- Use the debt-to-income ratio: Financial experts typically recommend keeping your debt-to-income (DTI) ratio at 40% or lower. Divide your total monthly debt payments by your gross monthly income to see where you stand.
- Consider the loan tenure: While longer tenures reduce your monthly EMI, they may increase the total interest paid over the life of the loan. Choose a tenure that balances monthly comfort with the total interest cost.
- Use online EMI calculators: Leverage the Bajaj Finance Personal Loan EMI Calculator to input different loan amounts, interest rates, and tenures. This helps you fine-tune your budget and plan your loan amount effectively.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the formula to calculate EMI?

The standard formula is E = P * r * (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1). Here, E is the EMI, P is the principal loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the loan tenure in months. You can use our online tools to simplify this calculation.

### How does the loan tenure affect the EMI you can afford?

A longer loan tenure generally results in a lower monthly EMI, making it easier to manage your budget. However, a longer tenure often means you will pay more in total interest over the life of the loan. It is important to find a balance that fits your current cash flow and your long-term financial goals.

### What is a good debt-to-income ratio to determine how much EMI is safe?

Financial experts generally suggest keeping your debt-to-income (DTI) ratio at 40% or lower. This ratio represents the percentage of your gross monthly income that goes toward paying off debts. Keeping this number low ensures that you remain in a strong position to handle future financial commitments.

### What are the benefits of a Flexi Loan?

A Flexi Loan allows you to withdraw funds from your sanctioned limit as needed and make prepayments whenever you have extra cash. You only pay interest on the amount you actually withdraw, rather than the entire sanctioned limit. This provides significant flexibility for managing variable financial needs.
